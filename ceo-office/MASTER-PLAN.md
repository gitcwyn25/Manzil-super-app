## 1. One paragraph

**Manzil** ("address" in Uzbek) is the trust layer for local life in Uzbekistan: consumers find and choose places through real reviews; businesses run their reputation, promotions, and customers from one dashboard — and pay for it. It is the Meituan/Dianping playbook — reviews first, transactions second, super-app last — executed in the youngest, fastest-digitizing market in Central Asia, where no local player owns "where should I go?"

## 2. Manzil at its peak

Close your eyes in 2031. A family in Yunusobod opens Manzil to pick a restaurant for a birthday — filtered by rating, live promos, and what neighbors said last week. They book a table, redeem a −20% set, and pay less than walk-ins. The owner sees the booking, the review that follows, and answers it before bed. A tourist in Samarkand does the same in English. A barber in Chilonzor opens their phone to a week of bookings that came from search.

At peak, Manzil is:

- **The default consumer answer** to "where?" in urban Uzbekistan — 5M+ MAU across all major cities plus the Silk Road tourist corridor.
- **The operating system for 25–50k service SMEs** — listing, reputation, promotions, bookings, customer base, statistics.
- **A transaction platform**, not a directory: vouchers, deal-sets, and bookings redeemed in-store, with take-rate and advertising revenue larger than subscriptions.
- **The trust monopoly**: the review graph nobody can copy, protected by a structural rule — *reviews are never removable for payment*.

## 3. The Meituan benchmark — and what Manzil brings

Meituan proves the model we are running. It merged with Dianping (China's review platform) in 2015 and built the world's largest local-services company on one flywheel: *reviews create trust → trust concentrates demand → demand gives merchants a reason to pay → merchant offers deepen consumer habit.* At its peak Meituan passed a $200B market capitalization on ~770M annual transacting users, with in-store (reviews + vouchers — the Dianping half) as its profit engine.

| Dimension | Meituan / Dianping | Manzil |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home market | China, 1.4B | Uzbekistan, ~37M — youngest big market in the region |
| Entry wedge | Group-buy deals; Dianping's reviews | Reviews + merchant SaaS (live today) |
| Monetization order | Deals → ads → delivery → fintech | SaaS → vouchers/bookings → ads → fintech-adjacent |
| Delivery | Built own logistics (capital-heavy) | **Partner-first** — Yandex/Uzum already burn capital here; we own choice, not couriers |
| Merchant relationship | Commission-led | Subscription-led (predictable), commission layered on top |
| Lesson we import | Win frequency, monetize trust | Same — at a scale one team can actually win |

**What Manzil brings** — to consumers: choice with confidence, prices that reward planning. To merchants: a customer channel they own, not rent from an algorithm. To Uzbekistan: SME digitization, formalized service commerce, a data backbone for a tourism economy the state is actively growing.

## 4. Business model

Two-sided platform, deliberately sequenced:

1. **Audience (free, forever):** consumer discovery + reviews — web today, iOS/Android next. The audience is the product the merchant pays for.
2. **Merchant SaaS (live):** Free tier to claim and reply (the wedge) → **Pro 399 000 UZS/mo** (promotions, gallery, advanced stats) → **Max 499 000 UZS/mo** (search priority, branches, dedicated manager). Pricing is admin-set data — repriceable without a deploy.
3. **Transactions (2027):** vouchers, deal-sets, bookings; 5–10% take-rate via Payme/Click rails.
4. **Advertising (2028):** clearly-labelled promoted placement; never touches organic review ranking.

Revenue projection (detail in FINANCE.md): $19k → $169k → $759k → $2.4M → **$6.0M** (2026→2030); breakeven during 2028–29; peak funding need ≈ $325k.

## 5. Market

- **TAM (2030):** ~$250M/yr — marketing + software + transaction take for Uzbekistan service SMEs (est. 200k+ businesses with walk-in custom) as O2O spend forms.
- **SAM:** ~$45M/yr — urban service businesses in the six largest cities.
- **SOM (5-yr):** ~$6M revenue = 10k paying merchants + take-rate — matching the projection above.

Tailwinds: median age ~29; internet penetration above 80% and climbing; Payme/Click made QR payment normal; the state pushes SME digitization and tourism (IT-Park tax incentives apply to us directly). *All figures are internal estimates for planning, marked as such.*

## 6. Porter's Five Forces

| Force | Pressure | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rivalry | **Moderate–high** | 2GIS/Yandex/Google own maps-utility; Uzum owns e-commerce. Nobody owns reviews-and-relationship for service SMEs — the Dianping seat is empty. |
| New entrants | **Moderate** | Code is cheap to copy; the review graph, seeded density, and doorstep merchant relationships are not. Global players deprioritize a 37M market — our window. |
| Buyer power — consumers | **High** | Free product, zero switching cost. Counter: review depth nobody else has, deals nobody else aggregates. |
| Buyer power — merchants | **Moderate** | Price-sensitive SMEs; the free tier removes resistance, public reviews create the *need* to engage. |
| Substitutes | **High — the real enemy** | Telegram groups, Instagram, word of mouth. Unstructured, unsearchable, unanswerable — we formalize what they already do. |
| Suppliers | **Low–moderate** | Cloud is commodity; payment rails are few (Payme/Click/Uzcard) — we integrate multiple rails from day one. |

## 7. SWOT

**Strengths** — live product (web SaaS + admin + API + dynamic pricing); trilingual by construction; review-integrity policy as brand; lean cost base (Tashkent engineering); founder domain proximity.
**Weaknesses** — solo-founder capacity; no native mobile apps yet; cold-start review inventory; unfunded; no operations team yet.
**Opportunities** — empty Dianping seat; SME digitization + IT-Park incentives; tourism boom (Samarkand/Bukhara/Khiva vertical); mature payment rails; Meituan-proven playbook to execute, not invent.
**Threats** — Yandex/Uzum adjacency expansion; copycats after we prove it; review fraud; UZS depreciation; scarce local venture capital; data-regulation shifts.

## 8. Challenges → how we overcome them

| Challenge | Our answer |
| --- | --- |
| **Chicken-and-egg** | Supply first, district by district. Listings are pre-seeded ("claim your page" converts better than "sign up"); win Chilonzor completely before touching the next district; consumers always see a full map. |
| **Trust / review fraud** | Verified accounts, moderation queue with audit trail (built), rate anomaly detection (planned), and the structural rule: no removal for payment — including from us. |
| **SME digital literacy** | Meet owners in Telegram: claim flow, notifications, and the CRM bot live where they already work. Doorstep onboarding closes what apps can't. |
| **Monetization timing** | Free tier until *their* customers create the pull (a public review demands a reply); upsell on evidence — weekly stats, not promises. |
| **Big-player entry** | Speed + depth: own the review graph and the merchant relationship before it's worth their while; partner for delivery instead of fighting logistics wars. |
| **Talent** | Meaningful equity (ESOP 10%+), IT-Park tax regime, a mission engineers can see live in their own city. |
| **Capital scarcity** | Small raises, visible unit economics (< $40 CAC, < 2-month payback), breakeven by design in 2028–29 — fundable in any climate. |

## 9. Goals

**Short-term (→ Q4 2026):** 1 000 claimed listings (≥300 in Chilonzor), 100 paying merchants, 10k consumer MAU, 5k reviews; Payme billing live, Telegram CRM bot beta, mobile apps public beta; close ~$200k pre-seed; sign CTO co-founder + growth lead.

**Long-term (2029–2031):** all major cities; 25–50k paying merchants; transactions + ads > 50% of revenue; operating profit from 2029; tourist vertical live; Series A/B for speed; evaluate one adjacent market only after Uzbekistan is clearly won.
